# GovCon SLED (State / Local / Education)

Your 22 agents assume the federal FAR world. **SLED is a different game** — no FAR, 50+ sets of state
rules, and local codes on top. For PSS's construction/civil clients, SLED (state DOT, municipal
infrastructure, K-12/university facilities) is often the **fastest bridge revenue** and the first real
past performance — exactly the Phase-2 move in the growth model.

## How SLED differs from federal

- **No FAR.** Each state has its own procurement code; counties, cities, and districts layer their own
  ordinances. Rules, thresholds, and forms vary by jurisdiction — read the local code, don't assume
  federal analogs.
- **Registration is per-jurisdiction.** Register on each state/local eProcurement portal (e.g., state
  vendor registries, city supplier systems) — there's no single SAM.gov equivalent, though SAM/UEI may
  still be needed for federally-funded local projects.
- **Method varies:** IFB/sealed bid (low-price, common for construction), RFP (best value), RFQ, and
  small-purchase thresholds that differ by jurisdiction. Many construction awards are **low
  responsive-responsible bidder**, so compliance and price discipline dominate.
- **Preferences are common:** local/in-state preference, resident-business preference, and
  disadvantaged/minority/women/veteran business programs (state DBE/MBE/WBE/VBE) — often *different*
  certifications than the federal ones. Check reciprocity.
- **Labor rules:** many states have **"little Davis-Bacon"** prevailing-wage laws for public works
  (and some cities have their own); federally-funded local projects still trigger federal Davis-Bacon.
  Confirm which wage determination applies.
- **Bonding:** bid/performance/payment bonds are standard on public construction, often triggered by
  state "Little Miller Act" thresholds. Route to the Bonding & Financial agent early.

## Cooperative purchasing (the scale lever)

Cooperatives let a public entity buy off an already-competed contract — win once, sell to many members
with minimal re-competition. Major vehicles: **Sourcewell, OMNIA Partners, NASPO ValuePoint, TIPS,
E&I** (education), and state/regional co-ops. GSA's **Cooperative Purchasing / 1122 Program** lets some
state/local buyers use federal sources for specific categories.

- Strategy: get **on** a cooperative contract (or team with a holder), then market to member agencies
  — it collapses the sales cycle. Mirrors the federal "piggybacking / IDIQ" play, adapted to SLED.

## Finding & pursuing SLED work

- Sources: state/local eProcurement portals and bid boards, state DOT project letting schedules,
  district/university purchasing pages, and aggregators (e.g., BidNet/Periscope, state registers).
- Relationship-building still wins: SLED buyers, like federal, work with those they know/like/trust —
  the APMP capture doctrine applies. Build the relationship before the IFB drops.
- Watch for **funding source:** local projects funded by **federal dollars** (DOT/FHWA, EPA SRF, IIJA
  pass-through) carry federal strings (Davis-Bacon, Buy America, SAM/UEI, Uniform Guidance) even though
  the buyer is state/local. Identify the money before you bid.

## How this plugs into the CRM

- **Business Readiness** — SLED is the Phase-2 bridge-revenue and first-past-performance path; sequence
  it before/alongside federal.
- **Market Research / BD** — add state/local portals and co-op vehicles to the opportunity sources.
- **Bonding & Financial** — Little Miller Act bonds on public construction.
- **Compliance** — swap FAR checks for the relevant state/local code; flag federal strings on
  federally-funded local work.

## Anti-patterns

- Assuming FAR rules apply to a state/city bid (they don't — read the local code).
- Missing that a local project is federally funded (Davis-Bacon/Buy America/UEI still apply).
- Ignoring cooperative vehicles (the fastest SLED scale path).
- Treating every state DBE/MBE cert as equivalent — they're jurisdiction-specific.
- Under-pricing a low-bid IFB without the bonding and prevailing-wage costs baked in.
