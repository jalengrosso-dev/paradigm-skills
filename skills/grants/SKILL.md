# GovCon Federal Grants & Cooperative Agreements

Grants are **not contracts** and don't run on the FAR. They fund a public purpose (assistance) rather
than buying a deliverable for the government, and they live under **2 CFR Part 200 (Uniform Guidance)**.
Relevant to PSS because civil/infrastructure clients can tap **IIJA/infrastructure** and agency
formula/discretionary funds — often as a subrecipient to a state/local entity.

> **Contract vs. grant vs. cooperative agreement:** a *contract* buys goods/services for the
> government (FAR). A *grant* funds a recipient's public-purpose project with little federal
> involvement. A *cooperative agreement* is a grant **with substantial federal involvement** during
> performance. Grants and co-ops both follow 2 CFR 200.

## The instruments & where to find them

- **NOFO / FOA** (Notice of Funding Opportunity / Funding Opportunity Announcement) — the grant
  equivalent of a solicitation; read it like an RFP (eligibility, priorities, review criteria, match,
  reporting).
- **Grants.gov** — central find/apply portal; agencies also run their own systems (e.g., DOT, EPA).
- Eligibility often limits **prime** applicants to states/locals/tribes/nonprofits — a for-profit
  civil firm frequently participates as a **subrecipient or contractor to the grantee**. Identify your
  eligible role early.

## The application package

- **SF-424 family** — SF-424 (cover), 424A/424C (budget: non-construction / construction), 424B/D
  (assurances), plus program-specific forms.
- **Registrations** — active **SAM.gov / UEI** is required to apply and receive funds; some programs
  require additional registrations. Start early (SAM can take days).
- **Project narrative** — need/problem, approach, outcomes, and evaluation, mapped **directly to the
  NOFO's review criteria and points** (same discipline as an APMP-compliant proposal: shred the NOFO,
  answer every criterion, lead with impact).
- **Budget & budget narrative** — every cost justified and tied to the scope; must be **allowable,
  allocable, and reasonable** under 2 CFR 200 Subpart E.

## Money mechanics that trip people up

- **Indirect cost rate:** use your **NICRA** (negotiated rate) if you have one, or the **de minimis
  rate — 15% of modified total direct costs** under the 2024 Uniform Guidance revision (raised from
  10%; **verify the current figure and effective date**, and confirm the program allows de minimis).
- **Cost share / match:** many infrastructure NOFOs require a non-federal match (cash or in-kind).
  Match must itself be allowable and documented; you generally **can't use other federal funds** as
  match unless a statute allows it.
- **Allowable costs:** Subpart E governs what's chargeable; watch unallowables (entertainment, most
  lobbying, alcohol, some travel). Pre-award costs need authorization.
- **Single Audit:** a non-federal entity expending **≥ $1,000,000** in federal awards in a year
  triggers a Single Audit under 2 CFR 200 Subpart F (**threshold raised in the 2024 revision from
  $750k — verify current**). Budget for the compliance burden.
- **Buy America / Davis-Bacon** apply to most federally-funded infrastructure — bake them into scope
  and price.

## Contract vs. grant decision

Prefer a **grant/co-op** when the work is a public-purpose project (infrastructure, resilience,
workforce) where your client (or a partner state/local) is an eligible recipient and match is
feasible. Prefer a **contract** when the government is directly buying your services. Often the play is
**subcontracting to a grantee** — combine this skill with Teaming.

## How this plugs into the CRM

- **Market Research / BD** — add Grants.gov and agency NOFO feeds; watch IIJA/agency infrastructure
  programs (verify current openings — these change by fiscal year and appropriation).
- **Proposal Builder / APMP** — a NOFO shreds and answers like an RFP; reuse the compliance-matrix
  discipline against the review criteria.
- **Teaming / Business Readiness** — structure the subrecipient/contractor role; confirm SAM/UEI and
  match capacity.
- **Compliance/Risk** — 2 CFR 200 (not FAR), Single Audit exposure, match documentation, Buy America.

## Anti-patterns

- Treating a NOFO like a FAR RFP (different rules, eligibility, and cost principles).
- Assuming a for-profit can prime a grant that's limited to states/nonprofits.
- Quoting a stale de minimis rate or Single-Audit threshold (both changed in 2024 — **verify**).
- Using federal funds as match without statutory authority.
- Forgetting Buy America / Davis-Bacon on federally-funded infrastructure.
- Ignoring the Single-Audit burden when expenditures cross the threshold.
